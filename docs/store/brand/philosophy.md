# Field & Signal — design philosophy of the Peek & Find / Pebblit marks

**Field & Signal.** A calm field of equal forms and one signal inside it that has been found.
Every mark in this family is built from that single tension: the many that rest, the one that
is seen. Nothing announces itself; the eye is led, not pushed. The work must look like it was
made slowly, by a hand that placed each form, weighed each gap and returned to it many times
until the composition held still on its own.

**Space and form.** Forms are flat, rounded, close to pebbles: shapes that have been handled.
They sit on a deep, quiet ground with generous air around them; the ground is part of the
drawing, not a backdrop. Within a mark the forms share one family of radii and one scale
rhythm, so the field reads as a single material. Composition follows the rule of thirds with a
deliberate off-centre weight: the signal sits where a searching eye arrives, never where it
starts. Edges are clean; no outline carries the shape, the colour does.

**Colour and material.** One deep night-blue ground. A field of muted, equal-luminance tones
that belong together like stones in a stream bed. One accent, a warm lamp-yellow, used once per
mark and only for the signal: a ring that says *here*. A second warm or cool tone may support
the signal, never compete with it. No gradients, no shadows, no bevels; depth comes from
overlap and from the patience of placement. The restraint is the craft: every tone is
calibrated so the mark still reads when it is 64 pixels wide.

**Scale and rhythm.** Repetition builds the field; the signal breaks it once. Sizes step in a
quiet ratio rather than at random, and the spacing between forms is held constant so the
field breathes evenly. The accent ring is the only element allowed to touch the rhythm from
outside: slightly larger than the form it holds, with a stroke heavy enough to survive
reduction and light enough to stay a line, not a badge.

**Typography.** Words are rare and set with the same care as the forms: a geometric,
low-contrast sans whose counters echo the pebble shapes, heavy enough to sit in the field,
never shouting. When a letter can carry the signal (a dot, a counter), the ring moves into the
type and the word becomes part of the drawing. No taglines, no secondary lines; a name is a
form among forms.

**Craft.** The finished mark must look meticulously made: optical corrections on every
alignment, balanced negative space, consistent stroke weights across sizes, a composition that
survives the circle crop of a profile photo and the rounded square of an app icon. The product
of deep expertise and painstaking attention, the kind of work that took countless hours and
hides every one of them.
